155 Enquiries. Zero Replies. Stock on the Shelf.

An aviation business had a sales problem it did not know existed. Here is how it surfaced.

HAI Seed Lab · 1 November 2026 · 3 min read

An aviation parts business sells partly through online marketplaces, where buyers post the parts they need and sellers reply with an offer. Requests arrive all day, and the team answers them, sends quotes and follows up.

Then HAI looked at two weeks of marketplace requests and found 155 that had never received a reply.

In one day's sample, 11 of the 14 unanswered requests were for parts already sitting in the company's warehouse.

The strange part

The requests were not lost. Every one of them had arrived exactly where the marketplace sent it: straight into the company's system.

The team was watching a different inbox.

So the system had done its job, and the people had done theirs, and between the two there was a gap that nobody owned. Requests went in, landed somewhere correct and quiet, and waited.

How it surfaced

No single source showed the problem. The marketplace data held the requests. The stock records held the parts on the shelves. The price records held the history of prices. On its own, none of them looked wrong.

HAI put the three side by side. Lined up against each other, they showed which requests had no reply, which of those were for parts in stock, and where sales were quietly slipping away.

That is the whole trick, and it is not a clever one. The information was already inside the company. It had never been in the same place at the same time.

What the numbers do and do not say

It is tempting to turn 155 into a sales figure. We will not, and here is why.

A marketplace request is not an order. Buyers often post the same request to many sellers and buy from one. A fast, well-priced reply raises the chance of a sale; it does not guarantee one. And 11 of 14 is one day's sample, not a count of the whole two weeks.

So what was found is a set of opportunities the company was not taking part in, many of them for parts it already had. How much revenue a reply would have brought is unknown, and we would rather leave it unknown than guess.

The information was already inside the company. It had never been in the same place at the same time.

Why this kind of loss stays invisible

A sales report lists the quotes that were sent, the deals that were won and the deals that were lost. A request that never started a quote leaves no line in any of them. You cannot see it in the numbers, because it never became a number.

That is also why nobody asks about it. People ask about the problems they can see. A request sitting unanswered in a system nobody watches is, by definition, a problem nobody can see. Finding it takes something that looks without being asked.

A check you can run this week

Pick one channel where customers can reach you without talking to a person: a marketplace, a web form, a shared mailbox. Look at the last 14 days. Count the requests that got no reply from your team, then count how many of those were for something you stock.

If the second number is not zero, you have just found the same problem.
