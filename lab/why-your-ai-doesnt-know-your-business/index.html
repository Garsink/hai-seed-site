Your AI Is Brilliant. Why Doesn't It Know Your Business?

The strange gap between what AI knows about the world and what it knows about your company.

HAI Seed Lab · 1 November 2026 · 4 min read

Ask a modern AI to explain why central banks raise interest rates and you will get a clear answer. Ask it to find the bug in a few hundred lines of code and it will probably find it. Ask it to draft a contract clause, summarise a technical standard or plan a trip across three countries, and it will do a respectable job.

Then ask it why your company promised a customer a different delivery date three weeks ago.

It has no idea. The answer was never written anywhere it can see. It lives in a meeting, a phone call, an email thread with a subject line about something else, and the memory of one colleague who happens to be on holiday.

This is the paradox of AI at work in 2026. Almost all of its intelligence is general. Almost all of the knowledge a business runs on is specific.

What a company actually knows

Most of a company's useful knowledge lives in its exceptions, and exceptions rarely make it into documents.

The standard payment terms are in the contract. The fact that one customer always pays two weeks late, always pays in full, and gets offended if you chase them on day one, is not. The price list is in the system. The reason a certain supplier gets a better price, because they once saved an urgent order on a Sunday, is not. The delivery terms are in the order. Everybody in logistics knows that one customer's warehouse closes at noon on Fridays, so anything arriving after eleven waits until Monday.

A new employee takes months to learn this. They learn it by being corrected, by overhearing, by getting it wrong once. Nobody hands them a file called how things actually work here, because no such file exists.

An AI joins the company in the same position, except it does not overhear anything.

Almost all of its intelligence is general. Almost all of the knowledge a business runs on is specific.

Why pasting it in does not work

The usual fix is to paste the context into the chat. Copy the email thread, attach last month's figures, explain the situation in three paragraphs, then ask the question.

It works, in the way that carrying water in your hands works. It depends on the person knowing what matters before they ask, which is exactly the thing they do not know when the question is hard. Different people paste different parts and get answers that disagree.

Many assistants now remember earlier conversations and can search connected files, and that helps. Conversation memory is still a record of what people happened to tell the AI. A company needs a different record: what actually happened, kept current and checked against the systems where it happened.

The more capable the AI becomes, the more obvious this gets. When anyone can get a fast general answer, the questions left are the specific ones: what should I decide this week, which customer is drifting away, what did we promise and not deliver. Those questions are made of company knowledge.

Company intelligence is a different thing

General intelligence is what a model brings. Company intelligence is what a business accumulates: what happened, who promised what, which numbers can be trusted, which rules have quiet exceptions, what went wrong last time and why.

It has some awkward properties. It changes every day. It contradicts itself, because two systems often disagree. Half of it is unwritten. And its most valuable parts are exceptions, which is exactly what a general model is trained to smooth over.

You do not get it by buying a smarter model. You get it by keeping a company's own record, current and checked, and letting the AI work from that.

What this means in practice

A company AI has to be built around one company. It needs a memory that stays current without someone pasting it in, answers that show where they came from, and a way to be corrected so that the correction holds. That is what we build HAI Seed to do.

A smarter model arrives every few months whatever you do. The company's own knowledge has to be built, and the sooner it starts, the more of it there is.
