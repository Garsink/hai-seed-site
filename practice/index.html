A KPI improved. Did the business?

From the founder’s HAI system.

Overdue receivables look better this week. Why?

Connected context
The receivables ledger, its change history, bank records and earlier meeting commitments.
What HAI found
A payment due date had been moved. The debt was still open and no payment had arrived. Separately, a cash-in total included customer advances.
What the owner gained
A clearer picture of actual collections and a specific payment-terms question for the management meeting.

Find the reason behind a change.

An early customer’s own HAI Seed setup.

Why has this recurring service cost gone up?

Connected context
The company’s declared quantities, cost records and earlier correspondence with the provider.
What HAI found
According to the owner, the increase came from higher declared quantities, not a price rise. HAI also brought back earlier correspondence about reviewing alternative providers.
What the owner gained
The cause of the change and its history in one place, giving the owner a concrete basis for the next conversation.

Asked in one line.

In the founder’s company, nine one-line questions were put to HAI on a Sunday morning, one for each part of the business: purchasing, sales, money, logistics, quality, people, contracts, market, and the leadership table. No data was exported or cleaned for it, and nothing in any system was changed. HAI read the systems, mail and files the company already had, and within forty minutes all nine answers were on file: the joined picture, the one thing no single system could show, how many records had no entry, and a decision for a person to take.

In most cases the system had a field for the answer, and it was rarely filled.

  1. Why are we losing deals, and what one change would win the most back?

    HAI read the lost quotes against the customers’ own emails. Most quotes marked lost had not been lost to a customer; a nightly job had expired them, and in the four largest losses where the customer had written back, the reason on file matched the customer’s words in none.

    Proposed: no priced quote is closed as lost without an outcome read from the customer’s thread and confirmed by a person. The head of sales decides.

  2. Which suppliers are quietly costing us customers?

    HAI lined up the orders, the receipts, the suppliers’ own offers and the quotes the company had lost. Four suppliers that quote days were delivering in weeks, and a quarter of the quote lines lost or declined for lead time had a faster offer already sitting in the company’s own files.

    Proposed: record each supplier’s promised date on the order and measure them against it, then change who gets the first call. The head of purchasing decides.

  3. Where do our orders lose days between the customer’s yes and the aircraft?

    HAI matched the orders to the warehouse mailbox and the forwarders’ pickups, as far as the truck. On the orders that could be traced the goods were ready in a day or two; most of the lost week sat in the hand-offs around the paperwork, where the system’s clock does not run.

    Proposed: issue the paperwork the day it is requested and tell the forwarder within twenty-four hours. The head of logistics decides.

  4. What is going to bite us in the next 60 days?

    HAI read the contract archive against the ERP’s approval clocks, this month’s quotes and the mail of the last quarter. The archive saw one expiry coming; the ERP saw dozens, the first within days.

    Proposed: a one-week sprint, no cash. The owner decides.

  5. Where is demand for what we sell in the next two years, and whom have we never spoken to?

    HAI joined a fleet forecast with the quote history, the show lists and the page invitations. Two fifths of the operators with work due had no two-way contact on record, and the largest of them was in the region of the trade show the team was flying to that week.

    Proposed: ten names, ten owners, one quarter. The CMO and the head of sales decide, one name each.

  6. What really made money last month?

    HAI joined the ledger, every invoice line and the date each bill was entered. The first read of the month was provisional: a cost from an earlier sale landed a week after month end, and the biggest sale earned its fee in the following month.

    Proposed: a hard date after which nothing moves into a closed month, and a decision on what to set aside. The CFO decides the treatment; the owner the provision.

  7. Who do we owe a decision to in the next 30 days, and who is at risk of leaving?

    Counts only; names stay with HR. HAI joined the people register, the HR calendar, the meeting record, the owner’s question queue and the volume of mail each person handles. Ten decisions about people were due inside thirty days, only one of them a probation review, and three people showed signs of strain that no single source would show.

    Proposed: one meeting this week. The owner decides.

  8. What will our customers and the auditor find before we do?

    HAI lined up supplier approvals, orders, returns and two years of supplier scorecards and listed what the audit would sample first: the approvals due for renewal before it, the receiving records with nothing written at inspection, and one supplier whose rating, approval and open return had never been read together.

    Proposed: a sprint to renew approvals and three corrective actions. The quality manager decides.

  9. What did we promise each other, what moved since, and what should I decide this week?

    HAI read the meeting record against mail, chat and the ledgers. Half the commitments had evidence behind them, three had evidence against them, and a customer’s reply had overtaken one of them while the tasks to deliver it stayed open.

    Proposed: three decisions with options. The owner decides.

HAI rebuilt each answer from mail, documents and ledgers the company already owned, and said how many records had no entry.

Real work from the founder’s HAI Seed deployment, anonymised. People as roles, companies unnamed, no money figures, counts rounded. Sources were connected with the owners’ consent. Times from the run logs cover the analysis; the review passes came after. Results depend on the sources connected and the workflow configured.
