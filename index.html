Run the businessReconstructed from real work. Details anonymised.

The owner asks: What should I decide this week?

Checked last week’s commitments against

  • Meeting notes
  • Customer email
  • ERP orders
  • Accounting
  • Management chat
  • Open decisions

HAI

Two decisions need you.

  1. A customer can no longer buy, but three tasks still chase the old plan.
  2. A deal we won depends on a supplier we still owe.

HAI recommends, you decide

  • Give the customer one dated answer, or release them in writing.
  • Settle the supplier first, then sublease instead of buying.

Next: each manager’s questions, ready for the weekly meeting sample

See how HAI got there
  • The customer’s latest reply changed what was possible. The tasks in the meeting record did not change.
  • We were about to ask the supplier for a new arrangement while an overdue balance was still open.
  • Done is not done: two items marked done show no change in the ERP since early October, and three marked open are already done.

Not read in this run: five managers’ mailboxes. HAI says what it could not see.

Find the next opportunityReconstructed from real work. Details anonymised.

The owner asks: Where should we focus our next sales push?

Matched two years of demand against

  • Market demand forecast
  • ERP accounts
  • Quotes and deals
  • Customer email
  • Show plans
  • Outreach records

HAI

Our biggest gaps are not where we are looking.

  1. Some of our strongest opportunities sit outside our current focus, including a prospect we could approach around an event already on the calendar.
  2. An account in the ERP is not a relationship: some are bulk imports or one-way offers.
  3. Some real conversations never reached the ERP.

HAI recommends, sales decides

  • Ten prospects, ten owners, one quarter: a brief for each, then a first human contact this month.

Waiting for the sales decision

See how HAI got there
  • Demand comes from a fleet forecast for the next two years, joined with our account, quote and conversation records.
  • Accounts created in bulk within minutes, with no reply ever, count as untouched.
  • Three airlines had written to us by email but had no record in the ERP.

Part of the list was re-checked live; the rest relies on an older snapshot, and HAI marks which is which.

Free up cashReconstructed from real work. Details anonymised.

The owner asks: Where is our money stuck with suppliers?

Matched supplier deposits against

  • Bank payments
  • Accounting
  • Purchase orders
  • Signed order terms
  • Supplier email

HAI

A refund we are owed, and an unnecessary repeat payment about to go out.

  1. Our own order terms make one deposit refundable. The supplier asked what we wanted five weeks ago; nobody replied.
  2. Goods had arrived without a matching supplier bill, leaving a risk of paying again.
  3. Some suppliers hold our deposits while we owe them invoices. These can be offset.

Decided

  • The action sheet went to finance, purchasing and sales the same day.

Every action has an owner and a date

See how HAI got there
  • An unnecessary repeat payment was scheduled for goods already paid. Switching it off takes two minutes.
  • The refund condition is in our own purchase order; the supplier’s question sat unanswered in email.

One supplier balance could not be explained from the live systems. HAI flagged it for the archived records instead of guessing.

Find the real causeReal HAI Seed use at another company. Details anonymised.

The owner asks: Why do our costs with this supplier keep rising?

Compared

  • Supplier invoices
  • Declared quantities
  • Email history

HAI

The rate stayed the same. The declared quantity rose.

  1. The price per unit has not changed.
  2. The quantity declared for billing increased, which explains the higher charge.
  3. The owner had assumed a price rise. The cause was different.

HAI recommends, you decide

  • Ask the supplier how quantities are measured and billed, and compare reporting periods.

About ten minutes to the answer, as the owner described it

See how HAI got there
  • Invoices and declared quantities, period by period, read together with the email thread about them.
  • Run in the company’s own HAI Seed, set up from its own records.

Prepare the negotiationBased on real HAI-assisted work for another business. Details anonymised.

The owner asks: Help me prepare for this negotiation.

Rebuilt the project history from

  • Signed contract
  • Design changes
  • Quantity and price lists
  • Completion records
  • Email
  • Site photos

HAI

Part of your claim is strong. Part would weaken it.

  1. Some extra work is backed by the customer’s own written approvals.
  2. Contracted work is missing from your claim.
  3. A billing mismatch on our side and unverified quantities need fixing before anything is sent.

HAI recommends, you decide

  • Fix the two weak points first, then send the revised letter.

Prepared: evidence register, revised letter, checks to do first

See how HAI got there
  • Some claimed quantities were purchases, not installed work.
  • Design changes after signing were matched to the approvals that allowed them.

No outcome is claimed here. The negotiation stays the owner’s.
