A component history for the engineer

The owner asked: Review the records for these three landing gears.

Before
The seller’s record set: 285 documents and 7,787 pages, over a quarter of them scans.
HAI connected
Every page, read and sorted by document type, then each component traced back to when it was made.
What it produced
A connected history for each of 127 components, 604 release certificates checked, and a short list of missing documents and left-right mismatches.
What the owner could do next
Give the list to the engineer, who makes the call, and ask the seller for the missing papers.

In the founder’s own use, HAI has prepared this kind of history in around two hours, where the manual work took an engineer several days per component. The engineer verifies the findings.

The real cause of a rising cost

The owner asked: Why do our costs with this supplier keep rising?

Before
The supplier’s charges kept climbing, and the owner assumed a price rise.
HAI connected
The supplier’s invoices, the quantities declared for billing and the email history about them, period by period.
What it produced
The rate per unit had not changed; the declared quantity had risen. HAI also brought back earlier correspondence about reviewing other providers.
What the owner could do next
Ask the supplier how quantities are measured and billed, with the history in hand. About ten minutes to the answer, as the owner described it.

A negotiation position built on evidence

The owner asked: Help me prepare for this negotiation.

Before
A table of extra work to claim, and a project history spread across many files.
HAI connected
The signed contract, design changes, quantity and price lists, completion records, email and site photos.
What it produced
An evidence register and a revised letter: what the customer’s own approvals support, contracted work missing from the claim, and two weak points to fix first, one of them a billing mismatch on the owner’s side.
What the owner could do next
Fix the weak points, then send the revised letter. No outcome is claimed here; the negotiation stays the owner’s.

People stay in control.

In the founder’s company, HAI reads widely and acts narrowly. Each outgoing action has its own permission, record and stop, and one master stop covers them all. HAI does not sign documents and does not move money.

The controls in detail
  • A message goes out only when its recipient matches in two independent places.
  • Messages with payment or bank instructions are held until a person releases them.
  • A financial figure is re-read from its source when a draft is written. If the source is stale, the figure is left out.
  • The ERP is read-only by default.
  • A new company starts with no outgoing actions and adds each one only when it approves it.
